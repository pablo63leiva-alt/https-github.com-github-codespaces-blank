Best Plumbing Tools for Apprentices

Updated September 14, 2026 · 10 min read · Tool Guides

The complete apprentice plumber starter kit — hand tools, power tools, soldering gear, safety equipment, and inspection cameras ranked by priority, with budget vs pro picks and real 2026 prices.

Disclosure: This page contains affiliate links. If you buy through these links, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. As an Amazon Associate, TradeLift earns from qualifying purchases.

Starting a plumbing apprenticeship and wondering what tools to put in your bag? The wrong move is buying everything at once — half of it won't be relevant for months. The right move is getting the 6-8 hand tools you'll use in your first week, then adding power tools, soldering gear, and specialty items as your skills and paychecks grow. Here's the complete plumbing tool list for apprentices, ranked by priority with budget and pro picks.

Here's the honest budget picture first: a complete apprentice plumbing setup runs about $180-$400 — $60-$120 for pipe wrenches, $15-$35 for a basin wrench, $15-$30 for a tubing cutter, roughly $20-$40 for a hacksaw and adjustable wrench, plus $50-$100 for a soldering kit, safety glasses, and gloves. That's a fraction of what a four-year degree costs, and it gets you working on day one, where entry-level plumbers start around $16-$20/hr and experienced plumbers pull $56K-$70K. Let's build your kit the right way.

Hand Tools: Your Everyday Essentials

Invest in good wrenches from the start — a slipping pipe wrench on a pressurized joint is dangerous and destructive.

1. Pipe Wrenches (Set of 2)

Why you need them: Two pipe wrenches are the foundation of plumbing. One holds the fitting steady while the other turns — you always work in opposing pairs to avoid stressing pipes. They grip threaded pipe and fittings for assembly, disassembly, and adjustments.

What to look for: Get a 14-inch and an 18-inch wrench to cover most residential and light commercial work. Look for heavy-duty cast iron housing, replaceable hardened jaws, and a smooth adjustment nut. Forged steel bodies resist cracking under the kind of leverage plumbers apply daily.

Price range: Budget (14" + 18" pair): $40-$70  |  Pro (Ridgid/Stillson): $80-$140

2. Basin Wrench

Why you need it: The basin wrench is the only tool that can reach the mounting nuts behind sink and toilet basins — the space where your hands and regular wrenches can't fit. It's the tool every apprentice reaches for on their first faucet install and immediately understands why it exists.

What to look for: A telescoping handle (extends to 16-17 inches for deep reach), a spring-loaded jaw that auto-grips, and a reverse-action mechanism so you can turn in tight spaces. A padded or rubberized handle reduces fatigue overhead.

Price range: Budget: $15-$25  |  Pro: $30-$50

Our Top Pick: Shop basin wrenches on Amazon

3. Tubing Cutter (Copper & PEX)

Why you need it: Clean, square cuts are critical for soldering copper and pushing PEX fittings. A tubing cutter gives you a perfect edge in seconds — no hacksaw burrs, no cracked pipe, no leaks. You'll use this tool every time you cut pipe.

What to look for: For copper, a compact wheel cutter with a wide capacity range (up to 1-1/8" or larger) covers most residential pipe. For PEX, a ratcheting PEX cutter slices cleanly in one squeeze. Some apprentice kits include both; if not, buy them separately.

Price range: Copper cutter: $15-$30  |  PEX cutter: $10-$20

Our Top Pick: Shop copper tubing cutters on Amazon  |  Shop PEX cutters on Amazon

4. Adjustable Wrench (Crescent)

Why you need it: Adjustable wrenches handle hex nuts, compression fittings, supply lines, and faucet connections — the parts of plumbing that pipe wrenches damage. Keep one on your belt at all times.

What to look for: A 10-inch adjustable wrench covers most residential plumbing nuts. Look for a wide jaw capacity (at least 1-1/4"), knurled adjustment for one-handed use, and a smooth jaw finish so you don't mar chrome fixtures.

Price range: Budget: $12-$20  |  Pro (Knipex/Crescent): $25-$50

Our Top Pick: Shop adjustable wrenches on Amazon

5. Hacksaw

Why you need it: When a tubing cutter can't reach the pipe — tight against a wall, under a cabinet, or on galvanized steel — the hacksaw is your backup. It also cuts PVC, strut, and bracket material that cutters aren't designed for.

What to look for: A 12-inch frame with a tensioning wing nut and a blade-change mechanism you can operate with gloves on. Pick up a multi-pack of bi-metal blades (18 and 24 TPI) — blades are consumable and a dull blade is dangerous.

Price range: Hacksaw frame: $10-$20  |  Blade multi-pack: $8-$15

Our Top Pick: Shop hacksaws on Amazon

6. Teflon Tape & Pipe Dope

Why you need them: Thread sealant is what makes threaded pipe joints watertight. Teflon tape (PTFE) wraps over male threads before assembly; pipe dope (thread sealant compound) fills the gaps. Most plumbers use both — tape first, then a thin coat of dope — for a joint that never leaks.

Price range: Teflon tape (multi-pack): $5-$10  |  Pipe dope: $6-$12

Our Top Pick: Shop Teflon tape on Amazon  |  Shop pipe dope on Amazon

Power Tools: Speed and Precision

Hand tools are the foundation, but power tools are what let a plumber work at professional speed.

7. Cordless Drill/Driver

Why you need it: A cordless drill shows up on nearly every rough-in and trim job — mounting pipe straps, drilling through studs, and driving screws for brackets. It's the first power tool every apprentice should own.

What to look for: An 18V or 20V brushless model with a chuck that accepts up to 1/2-inch bits, variable speed with a clutch, and at least two batteries so one is always charged. A hammer-drill mode is useful when you're drilling into concrete or masonry.

Price range: Budget (bare tool): $50-$90  |  Pro (Milwaukee/DeWalt): $120-$200

Our Top Pick: Shop cordless drills on Amazon

8. Reciprocating Saw (Sawzall)

Why you need it: Demolition and rough-in demolition — cutting old pipe out of walls, slicing through corroded galvanized, and trimming pipe in tight spaces. The reciprocating saw is the demolition tool of plumbing, and every apprentice will use one within their first month.

What to look for: A cordless 18V/20V model with tool-free blade change and orbital action (cuts faster). Buy a blade variety pack — bi-metal for pipe, carbide for nail-embedded wood, and fine-tooth for clean cuts on new pipe.

Price range: Budget (bare tool): $60-$100  |  Pro: $130-$200

Our Top Pick: Shop reciprocating saws on Amazon

Soldering Kit: Copper Pipe Joining

Soldering copper is one of the first skills you'll learn and the one that separates a plumber from a homeowner with a wrench. A proper soldering setup costs less than $50 and is non-negotiable.

10. Propane or MAPP Gas Torch Kit

Why you need it: You need heat to solder copper joints — not a flame, but a controlled, focused torch. MAPP gas burns hotter than propane and heats joints faster (especially on larger pipe), but propane is cheaper and fine for 1/2-inch to 3/4-inch residential work.

What to look for: A self-igniting torch head (no flint striker required), a stable base so it stands upright between uses, and a tank that fits comfortably in one hand. MAPP gas cylinders are yellow; propane are blue. A kit with the torch head and one cylinder is the best starting point.

Price range: Propane kit: $15-$30  |  MAPP kit: $25-$50

Our Top Pick: Shop soldering torch kits on Amazon

11. Lead-Free Solder & Flux

Why you need them: Lead-free solder is legally required on potable water lines in all 50 states. Flux cleans the joint surface and helps solder flow into the fitting by capillary action. Buy lead-free only — never use old-school lead solder on drinking water pipe.

Price range: Solder (1/2 lb roll): $10-$20  |  Flux: $6-$12

Our Top Pick: Shop lead-free solder on Amazon  |  Shop flux on Amazon

12. Emery Cloth & Pipe Brush

Why you need them: You can't solder dirty pipe. Emery cloth (or sandcloth) cleans the outside of copper pipe; a wire brush cleans the inside of fittings. Every joint starts with cleaning — skip it and you get a cold joint that leaks.

Price range: Emery cloth (multi-pack): $5-$10  |  Fitting brush set: $8-$15

Our Top Pick: Shop emery cloth on Amazon  |  Shop fitting brushes on Amazon

Safety Gear: Protect Yourself Daily

Sharp pipe edges, chemical drain cleaners, and limited-visibility crawl spaces make safety gear non-negotiable.

13. Safety Glasses

Why you need them: Cutting pipe, soldering overhead, drilling into walls, and working under sinks all send debris toward your eyes. ANSI-rated safety glasses are the minimum — goggles for overhead soldering are better.

Price range: $8-$15

Our Top Pick: Shop safety glasses on Amazon

14. Work Gloves

Why you need them: Pipe edges are razor-sharp, especially cut copper and old galvanized. Work gloves protect against cuts, abrasions, and chemical contact. You need dexterity for soldering, so go for snug-fitting mechanix-style gloves rather than bulky leather for most tasks.

Price range: Budget: $10-$18  |  Pro: $20-$35

Our Top Pick: Shop work gloves on Amazon

15. Knee Pads

Why you need them: You will spend hours on your knees — under sinks, on concrete, in crawl spaces. Knee pads aren't glamorous, but they're the difference between finishing your shift and limping home. Foam-padded gel knee pads that strap to your belt or fit in your pants pockets are the most practical for plumbing.

Price range: Budget: $10-$20  |  Pro (gel/reinforced): $25-$45

Our Top Pick: Shop knee pads on Amazon

Inspection Camera: See Inside the Pipes

16. Borescope / Inspection Camera

Why you need it: An inspection camera lets you look inside drains, walls, and under slabs without tearing anything open. For an apprentice, it's a learning tool — watching a camera snake through a drain teaches you more about plumbing systems than a textbook. For a journeyman, it saves hours of exploratory demolition.

What to look for: A 5-6mm camera head (fits into 1/2-inch pipe), at least 1080p resolution, a 3-5 meter cable, and WiFi or USB connection to your phone. Waterproof rating (IP67) is essential — this tool lives in water. Budget models under $100 are fine for learning; professional-grade units with self-leveling heads run $200-$500.

Price range: Budget: $50-$100  |  Pro: $200-$500

Our Top Pick: Shop inspection cameras on Amazon

17. Plumbing Snake / Drain Auger

Why you need it: Every plumber clears drains. A manual hand auger (snake) clears sinks, tubs, and floor drains for under $30 and fits in your tool bag. For larger drains, a motorized drain auger handles 2-4 inch lines.

What to look for: A 25-foot manual snake with an auto-feed mechanism and a bulb or arrowhead tip covers most residential drain calls. For a powered auger, look for variable speed and a drum that catches the cable.

Price range: Manual snake: $20-$40  |  Powered auger: $80-$200

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do plumbing tools cost for beginners?

A basic apprentice plumbing kit costs $180 to $400. That covers pipe wrenches, a basin wrench, a tubing cutter, a hacksaw, Teflon tape, a soldering kit, safety glasses, and work gloves. You can start with budget tools and upgrade your most-used wrenches and cutters as your skills and paychecks grow.

What tools does a first-year plumbing apprentice need?

First-year apprentices typically need two pipe wrenches, a basin wrench, a tubing cutter, a hacksaw, an adjustable wrench, Teflon tape, a soldering torch and solder, safety glasses, and work gloves. Many employers provide specialty tools — start with the basics and let the job guide what you buy next.

Should plumbing apprentices buy cheap or expensive tools?

Start with mid-range tools from reputable brands like Ridgid, Milwaukee, or Irwin. Ultra-cheap pipe wrenches strip threads and slip under pressure, which is both frustrating and dangerous. You don't need the most expensive professional line on day one, but avoid the absolute bottom tier — especially for wrenches and cutters that see daily use.

Do I need an inspection camera as a plumbing apprentice?

Not on day one, but it becomes valuable by your second or third year. An inspection camera (borescope) lets you see inside drains, walls, and under slabs without tearing anything open. Budget models in the $50-$100 range are fine for learning — you'll use it on drain troubleshooting and leak detection jobs constantly.

Your Next Step

You don't need a $2,000 tool truck to start — you need the right wrenches, a cutter, a torch, and the willingness to learn. Buy the Phase 1 kit, show up ready to work, and build your kit as your skills and paychecks grow. Every experienced plumber started with two pipe wrenches and a tubing cutter in a basic bag. The rest comes with time.
