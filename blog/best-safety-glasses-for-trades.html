Best Safety Glasses for Construction

Safety glasses are the cheapest piece of PPE on your harness, and the one most guys refuse to wear for more than the first week. Here's the thing: dust, flying concrete, wayward screws, and sparks don't care how many years you've been on a job site. Every year, thousands of eye injuries hit construction workers — and nearly all of them are preventable with glasses that cost less than your lunch.

You don't need a $200 pair of designer safety specs. You need ANSI Z87.1-rated lenses that fit your face, don't fog up mid-sand, and actually stay on your head while you're 12 feet up a ladder. We broke down the market into honest buckets — top pick, budget, best ANSI Z87.1 wrap-fit, best anti-fog, best over-glasses, and the best value pair pack — so you can grab the right pair and get back to work.

Why ANSI Z87.1 Matters (And What Z87+ Means)

When you see Z87.1 stamped on a lens, that's the American National Standard for occupational and educational personal eye and face protection. The 2020 revision is the current one most job sites reference. It's the difference between "these look like safety glasses" and "these are safety glasses."

The base Z87 mark covers basic impact: a steel ball dropped from 50 inches. The Z87+ high-impact mark covers high-mass and high-velocity impact — a heavier missile at higher speed. On a construction site with powered fasteners, grinding, and pressure washers, Z87+ is the one you want when you can get it.

The other thing the standard does is give you a UV rating. Look for the optional rating on the lens — a U6 label means it absorbs at least 99.9% of UV. Clear lenses protect against impact; they do not automatically block UV. If you spend summers adding a roof or pulling a cable across a parking lot, tinted or UV-marked lenses matter.

How to Choose Safety Glasses for the Job Site

Here's the short checklist that separates a pair you'll wear from a pair that lives in the truck:

Check the Mark, Not the Brand

Every pair of eyes deserves the same rule: the lens or frame must carry the Z87 marking. If it doesn't, it's not certified impact protection. For grinding, demolition, and power fastening, step up to Z87+ high-impact where it's available.

Anti-Fog Beats Everything on a Sweaty Job

Anti-fog is the single biggest reason glasses end up on top of a hard hat instead of over eyes. Look for baked-in anti-fog coating, ventilated frames, or breathable foam (common on anti-dust styles). Avoid antiques with no coating — or budget for that failure.

Coverage and Fit

  • Wrap-fit: Full side coverage against dust and angle debris. The pick for demolition, concrete, and grinding work.
  • Flat lens: Lighter and clearer optics for detail work like wiring and trim. Fine on tidy indoor sites.
  • Over-glasses (OTG): Fits over prescription specs. Test that the frame clears your lenses and stays put when you bend.

UV for Outdoor Sun, Clear for Indoor

Outdoor crews doing roofing, pavement, or long site walks should reach for tinted or UV-rated lenses (look for the U6 rating). Indoor, shop, and panel work favors clear lenses — better visibility, no adjustment time walking from daylight into a dim basement.

Care Tips: Make Them Last and Know When to Toss Them

Pro Tip: Safety glasses don't need much — wipe them with a clean microfiber cloth at the end of the shift, wash them with mild soap and water when they're actually dirty, and keep them in a soft case or a pocket you don't share with screws. The instant a lens gets scratched, pitted, or yellowed enough to blur your view, replace it. A scratched lens isn't "fine" — it's a distraction with a job site attached. At $3 to $30 a pair, there's no excuse to keep one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are all safety glasses ANSI Z87.1 certified?

No. ANSI Z87.1 certification is voluntary — a pair of glasses is only certified if the lens and frame carry the Z87 (or Z87+ for high impact) marking. Sunglasses without the mark are not impact rated, no matter how thick the lens looks. On a job site, look for the mark itself, not the brand name.

Do safety glasses protect your eyes from the sun?

Some do. Clear safety lenses protect against impact, but they block very little UV on their own unless the lens carries a UV rating. The Z87.1 standard includes an optional UV rating — a U6 lens absorbs at least 99.9% of UV. If you work outdoors on sunny sites, pick a tinted or UV-rated lens; if you're mostly indoors under harsh lights, clear anti-fog lenses are usually the better daily choice.

How do I keep my safety glasses from fogging up?

Anti-fog coating is the biggest help — many current models bake the coating into the lens, and that lasts longer than the spray-on kind. Ventilated frames and breathable foam, common on anti-dust styles, also cut fogging on sweaty days. Clean lenses with mild soap and water, not abrasive wipes and not your shirt, because scratches and solvents strip the coating faster.

Can I wear safety glasses over my prescription glasses?

Yes, with an over-the-glass (OTG) model — broad, loose wrap frames that fit over normal spectacles. Try the fit before you buy: the frame should sit clear of your prescription lenses and stay put when you bend over or look down. If a loose fit bothers you all day, prescription safety lenses or a removable internal prescription frame (RXR) are a cleaner route.

How long do safety glasses last?

There's no fixed expiry date, but a scratched, pitted, or yellowed lens should be replaced — impact-rated or not, a lens you can't see through clearly makes your work less safe, not more. Inspect your glasses before each shift, clean them properly, and when a pair is done, a budget pair costs less than a load of lumber. A scratched lens is not something to live with.
