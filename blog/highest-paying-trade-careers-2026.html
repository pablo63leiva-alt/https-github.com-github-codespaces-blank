The 10 Highest-Paying Trade Careers in 2026

Updated September 14, 2026 · 9 min read · Career Guide

Forget the myth that you need a four-year degree to earn six figures. Ten trade careers now pay $56K-$100K+ with just a license and experience. Here's the data-driven ranking, straight from the Bureau of Labor Statistics, plus how to get started in each one.

If you're searching for the highest-paying trade careers in 2026, you're ahead of the game — because millions of people still believe the outdated idea that good pay requires a four-year degree. The data says otherwise. Multiple skilled trade careers now pay $60K-$100K+ with just a registered apprenticeship, a license, and experience. No $100,000 tuition bill required.

This guide ranks the top 10 trade careers by 2026 salary, using U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics data and current industry projections. For each trade, you'll get the median pay, the growth outlook, and a clear answer on how to get started. Whether you're 17 or 27, this is the most data-backed comparison you'll find.

Why Trade Careers Pay More Than You Think

The median salary for all U.S. workers is roughly $48,000. The median for four-year college graduates sits around $60K-$65K. Multiple trade careers now match or beat that — without the $37,000+ in average student loan debt.

And the pay ceiling in the trades is higher than most people realize. Elevator installers clear $100K. Master electricians and plumbers hit $80K-$150K+. Welders in specialized industries push past $80K. Meanwhile, the Bureau projects strong demand across all of these fields for the rest of the decade — driven by retirements, infrastructure investment, and a massive skilled-worker shortage.

"The trades aren't Plan B anymore. For hundreds of thousands of young workers, they're the smarter financial move — high pay, zero debt, and demand that keeps climbing."

The full breakdown of how each trade stacks up — salary, growth, training time — is on our Trades page. This post goes deeper on the top 10 by pay.

1. Elevator Installer & Repairer — The Trade That Pays Over $100K

Elevator installers and repairers are the highest-paid trade workers in the U.S. with a median annual salary exceeding $100,000. The work involves installing, maintaining, and repairing elevators, escalators, and moving walkways in commercial and residential buildings.

The catch: the work is specialized, the apprenticeship is rigorous (typically 4-5 years), and you need to be comfortable with electrical systems, hydraulics, and mechanical controls. But for the right person, the payoff is unmatched in the trades. Entry requires a union apprenticeship through the IUEC (International Union of Elevator Constructors).

How to start: Apply through your local IUEC hall or check apprenticeship.gov. Strong math and mechanical aptitude will help you on the entrance test.

2. Electrician — $61K-$80K Median, $100K+ Ceiling

Electricians are among the most in-demand trades in the country, with the Bureau projecting 6% growth through 2033 — much faster than average. The median salary sits at $61,600, but union journeymen, industrial electricians, and master electricians routinely earn $80K-$100K+.

The growth drivers are structural: solar installations, EV charging infrastructure, smart building retrofits, and a massive retirement wave are all creating more openings than the pipeline can fill. Electricians can specialize in residential, commercial, industrial, or lineman work — each with its own pay premium.

How to start: 4-5 year registered apprenticeship through the IBEW or a non-union program. No degree required. See our full how to become an electrician guide for the complete step-by-step path.

3. Plumber, Pipefitter & Steamfitter — $56K-$74K, Master Plumbers $100K+

Plumbing is one of the trades with the highest long-term earning potential because of the master plumber license pathway. Median pay is $56K-$74K, but master plumbers who run their own businesses regularly clear $80K-$150K+ depending on market and operation size.

The demand drivers are simple: every building needs water, gas, and waste systems. Renovation, new construction, and aging infrastructure all drive steady work. Gasfitting, medical gas, and industrial piping are the highest-paying specializations.

How to start: 4-5 year registered apprenticeship through a UA local. See our step-by-step how to become a plumber guide for the full path, from high school prep to master license.

4. Welder — $50K-$65K Median, $85K+ in Specialized Industries

Welding is one of the fastest trades to enter — certifications can be earned in 7-18 months — and the pay ceiling is higher than most people expect. Median pay is $50K-$65K, but welders in specialized industries like pipeline, underwater, aerospace, and nuclear consistently earn $70K-$100K+.

The trade is also remarkably flexible: you can work in construction, manufacturing, shipbuilding, energy, automotive, or aerospace. Multiple welding certifications (MIG, TIG, stick, flux-core) each open different job markets.

How to start: Trade school or community college welding program (7-18 months), then earn AWS certifications. See our is welding a good career guide for the full breakdown.

5. HVAC Technician — $50K-$70K, Commercial Specialists $90K+

HVAC (heating, ventilation, and air conditioning) is one of the most recession-resistant trades — every building needs climate control regardless of the economy. Median pay is $50K-$70K, and technicians specializing in commercial refrigeration, building automation, or heat pump installation regularly push past $90K.

The Bureau projects 5% growth through 2033, driven by heat pump adoption, data center expansion, and the constant service demand for residential and commercial systems. EPA 608 certification is required to handle refrigerants and can be earned in weeks.

How to start: 3-5 year apprenticeship or a 6-24 month trade school program, plus EPA 608 certification. See our full HVAC apprentice salary guide for year-by-year pay.

6. Power Line Installer & Repairer — $78K-$85K Median

Power line workers (linemen) are among the highest-paid trade workers who don't run their own businesses. Median pay exceeds $78K, and overtime and storm-call premiums can push total compensation past $100K. The work involves installing and repairing high-voltage power lines and electrical infrastructure.

The trade demands physical fitness, comfort with heights, and willingness to travel for storm restoration work. It's demanding — and the pay reflects it. The BLS projects steady demand as the grid expands and existing lines require maintenance.

How to start: 3-year apprenticeship through a utility company or union program. Strong climbing ability and mechanical aptitude are essential.

7. Industrial Machinery Mechanic — $60K-$68K Median

Industrial machinery mechanics install, maintain, and repair manufacturing equipment — a critical role in every factory, plant, and production facility. Median pay is $60K-$68K, with experienced mechanics in heavy industry or automation earning $80K-$100K+.

The demand is growing as manufacturing automation increases. Programmable logic controllers (PLCs), robotics, and conveyor systems all need skilled mechanics who can diagnose and fix complex problems. The field blends mechanical skill with electronics and computer diagnostics.

How to start: 2-5 year apprenticeship or technical program. NIMS (National Institute for Metalworking Skills) and MSSC certifications add significant value.

8. Carpenter — $49K-$56K Median, $85K+ for Specialists

Carpentry is the broadest trade on this list — it spans framing, finish carpentry, cabinetmaking, concrete formwork, and millwork. Median pay is $49K-$56K, but finish carpenters, cabinetmakers, and carpenters in high-cost markets consistently earn $65K-$85K+.

Carpentry also has the most direct path to self-employment. Carpenters who build custom cabinetry, finish work, or remodeling businesses set their own rates and can scale quickly.

How to start: 3-4 year apprenticeship through the United Brotherhood of Carpenters or a non-union program. Strong math and spatial reasoning are key.

9. Boilermaker — $65K-$75K Median

Boilermakers assemble, install, and repair boilers and other large pressure vessels used in power plants, refineries, and industrial facilities. Median pay is $65K-$75K, and experienced boilermakers in high-demand industries earn $85K-$100K+.

The work is physically demanding and often involves travel, shutdowns, and confined spaces — but the specialized skill set commands premium pay. Boilermaker apprenticeships are among the most selective in the trades.

How to start: 4-5 year apprenticeship through the International Brotherhood of Boilermakers. Physical fitness and willingness to travel are requirements.

10. Wind Turbine Technician — Fastest Growing, $57K-$61K Median

Wind turbine techs are the fastest-growing occupation in the U.S., with the BLS projecting 45% growth through 2033. Median pay is $57K-$61K, and experienced techs in remote or offshore roles earn $75K-$85K+. The field is perfect for people who like climbing, mechanical work, and being outdoors.

The growth is driven by the massive expansion of wind energy across the country. As more wind farms come online, the demand for trained technicians keeps accelerating.

How to start: 2-year technical program or wind energy certificate, plus climbing safety training and first aid. Many community colleges now offer dedicated wind energy programs.

How to Pick the Right Trade for You

The highest-paying trade isn't always the best trade for you. The right career depends on your strengths, interests, and what you want your daily work to look like. Consider these factors:

  • Physical demands: Elevator installers and linemen do demanding physical work. Carpentry and welding are also physically active. If you want less physical strain, consider HVAC or industrial machinery.
  • Training time: Welding is the fastest entry (7-18 months). Electrician and plumber apprenticeships run 4-5 years. Wind tech programs take 2 years. Factor in your timeline and financial situation.
  • Geographic demand: Elevator installers cluster in cities. Linemen and boilermakers travel. HVAC techs are needed everywhere. Check what's hiring in your area.
  • Self-employment potential: Plumbing, electrical, carpentry, and HVAC all have strong self-employment paths. Welding and wind tech are more employer-based.
  • Specialization ceiling: Some trades have clear premium specializations — gasfitting in plumbing, solar/EV in electrical, pipeline in welding. The trades with more specialization lanes often have the highest long-term earning ceilings.

The Big Picture: Why These Paychecks Keep Rising

Every trade on this list shares two things: high demand and a shrinking supply of workers. The average skilled tradesworker is over 40. Hundreds of thousands retire every year, and not enough young workers are replacing them. That gap pushes wages up across the board.

Add in the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, the Inflation Reduction Act's clean-energy incentives, and a housing shortage that won't solve itself — and the demand side isn't slowing down. These aren't cyclical booms. They're structural shifts that will keep trade paychecks climbing for years.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the highest-paying trade career in 2026?

Elevator installers and repairers are the highest-paid trade workers in 2026, with a median salary of over $100,000 a year according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Industrial machinery mechanics, power line workers, and gas plant operators also regularly earn $80K-$100K+.

Do trade careers really pay as much as college careers?

Many trades match or exceed the median salary of four-year degree holders — and they do it with zero student debt. The median salary for college graduates is around $60K-$65K, while multiple trade careers now pay $60K-$100K+ with just an apprenticeship and a license.

What trade is easiest to get into with high pay?

Welding and HVAC are among the fastest trades to enter with strong pay. Welding certifications can be earned in 7-18 months and lead to $50K-$65K starting pay. HVAC apprenticeships run 3-5 years but pay $15-$30/hr from year one. Both fields have strong job openings and demand.

Which trade careers have the best job growth?

Wind turbine technicians, plumbers, electricians, and HVAC technicians are projected to grow much faster than the average occupation through 2033. The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects strong demand driven by infrastructure investment, retirements, renewable energy expansion, and a chronic shortage of skilled workers.

Can I start a trade career with no experience?

Yes. Most registered apprenticeships require only a high school diploma or GED, basic math skills, and a willingness to learn. No prior trade experience is needed — the apprenticeship exists to train you from zero. You earn while you learn, and your employer pays for your training.
