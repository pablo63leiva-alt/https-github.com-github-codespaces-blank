### Research checklist — per restaurant (10 minutes each)
Pull from the site, Facebook, Google Business Profile, and Instagram in this order. A restaurant that cannot be researched this way is a weaker pilot candidate — the point is they are reachable through normal local channels.

- [ ] **Name** — exact legal/trade name and the name on their sign
- [ ] **Owner / GM name** — look for the person, not the brand: Facebook "About" page, site "Our Story", Google photo captions, local news features
- [ ] **Phone** — main line; note quiet hours (usually 2-3 PM, between lunch and dinner service)
- [ ] **Email** — from site contact page or Facebook; if none, phone-first contact plan
- [ ] **Current loyalty / social presence** — do they have punch cards, a Toast/Square loyalty sign, an email capture, an active Instagram? Note what exists so we never pitch a duplicate
- [ ] **Their slow days** — Google busy-times graph is the fastest read; confirm with the owner in the first call
- [ ] **Famous menu item** — the thing they are locally known for; this becomes the free-appetizer offer anchor and the email name-drop (e.g., the burnt ends, the 32-oz margarita, the family hangover breakfast)
- [ ] **Free appetizer idea** — a low-cost, high-perceived-value item they already make (side-sized, no prep surge) for the QR offer
- [ ] **POS brand** — Square / Clover / Toast / other, so we can truthfully say "works with any POS, no lock-in"
- [ ] **Obstacles** — paper menus only? no wifi? bilingual signage needed? single owner who never reads email? Note the contact strategy accordingly.

### Contact plan per lead
1. Research (above) → 2. quiet-hours call (Tue/Wed 2-3 PM) → 3. email only if a real address exists → 4. follow-up day 7 → 5. no-reply closer day 14 → 6. drop even if they say "call me later" twice. Respect "no" the first time and leave the door open.

## 2. Outreach Email Template — Pilot Offer

Voice: peer-to-peer, local, specific to *their* restaurant. Short sentences. No hype words (no "revolutionize", "skyrocket", "limited time only").

### Subject lines (3 variants)
- `A Monday idea for [RESTAURANT] (no software work on your end)`
- `A free-appetizer sign that fills your slow Mondays`
- `Two minutes on your [hero menu item], [OWNER NAME]`


## 3. Phone / Voicemail Script + In-Person Walk-In

### Quiet-hours setup
- Call Tue/Wed **2-3 PM** (post-lunch, pre-dinner prep — the slowest window).
- Ask for the owner/GM by name from research.
- Have the hero menu item and offer ready; the call is 90 seconds, not a pitch.

### Phone script (live, ~90 seconds)

```
"Hi, is [Owner Name] around? ... Hi [Owner], this is Ramonda — I'm
not selling anything over the phone, I just wanted a yes-or-no on a
30-second idea. I help local restaurants like yours build a texting
list. You scan a QR sign on the table, guests opt in and get a free
[appetizer], and you own the list. We do all the setup and writing,
free for the first month. Could you use a filled-in Monday? If it
sounds worth one short call, I can send you a two-minute video.
If not, say the word and I'll drop it."

Possible replies:
- "How much?" → "$99 a month flat after a free first month. No
  contract. Your call."
- "We're too busy right now" → "That's exactly why I'll email
  instead — my note is coming, no need to reply. You'll have it."
- "Not interested" → "Totally fair. I won't bother you again. If
  Mondays ever change, TableText is easy to find. Enjoy your week."
```

### Voicemail (leave at most once)

```
"Hi [Owner Name], this is Ramonda from TableText. I help local places
like [RESTAURANT] turn a free-appetizer QR sign into a texting list,
so their Monday special actually reaches people. I did the research on
your [hero menu item], and I'll send one short email so you can look
it over whenever. Nothing to do in the voicemail. Thanks — my number
is [PHONE]."

```
Rules: never read a script verbatim like a recording; adapt to tone. If they sound rushed, offer the email and hang up warmly.

### 90-second in-person pitch (walk-in, when the manager is up front)

Say, in less than a minute and a half:

```
"Hi — I'll keep this to 90 seconds. [RESTAURANT]'s [hero menu item] is
a local favorite, and you're packed Fridays. I help local places fill
the slow days with a texting list. A QR sign on the table, guests text
their number, they get a free [appetizer], and you own the list. We
print the signs, write the texts, run it entirely — free for the first
month, then a flat $99. All I ask for a yes is a good slow-day offer
and your approval on two text drafts. Can I put a one-pager in your
hands and follow up with you Wednesday at 2?"

```

- Leave the pilot one-pager (section 4) printed on one page with your name, phone, and the QR offer mocked with *their* menu item.
- **What NOT to do at the walk-in:** do not pitch during peak service or while they are on a ticket; do not talk at the counter holding up the line; do not use a tablet or laptop; do not linger past 90 seconds; do not ask for a manager's number under false pretenses; do not leave for a competitor or mention "everyone else is signing up."

### Restaurant obligations (6 bullets)
- Provide one slow-day offer we can promote (approx. one appetizer/promo item) and approve every blast before it sends
- Pick where tents go and say yes/no on table coverage
- Servers do nothing new except answer "just scan it for a free [appetizer]" — no staff training beyond that
- Keep the phone number list accurate and report any signage damage on reorder
- Tell us immediately about complaints or STOPs so we fix them same-day
- Nothing to pay in month one; approve continued billing (if kept) before it ever charges

### TableText obligations (6 bullets)
- Disclose clearly that we are an independent service, not affiliated with their POS vendor or phone carrier
- Never send more than 2 texts per week to any guest (cap: 1 promo + welcome/confirm only)
- Own compliance: opt-in consent stored, STOP/HELP honored within one message, applicable laws followed
- Keep their content and numbers private: no sharing with third parties, no renting their list
- Report numbers honestly every week, including opt-outs and unsubscribes
- If the restaurant cancels, transfer everything and walk away cleanly

### Data ownership
- **The restaurant owns the guest list** — end of story. It transfers to them in a usable format on request or on cancellation.
- TableText keeps a copy for service continuity (so we can keep sending and honoring STOPs) and aggregated, anonymized insights (e.g., "pizza places see X% claim rates") — never individual phone numbers or names outside the service.
- Guests can remove themselves at any time with STOP, and both sides must honor it.

## 6. Feedback Loop — Week 2 and Week 4 Check-Ins

### The 5 questions (ask the owner / GM, 10 minutes, voice or in person)
1. **Signups/day** — "How many people scanned per night, roughly? Which night did the most?"
2. **Claim rate** — "Of the people who got the text, about how many came back to claim the free [appetizer]?"
3. **Table engagement** — "What did your guests say about the QR sign? Did servers find it annoying, invisible, or a talking point?"
4. **Blast response** — "What happened on the Monday blast? Any calls, replies, or walk-ins that mention the text?"
5. **Owner time spent** — "Honest number: how many minutes a week did this cost you outside the check-in calls?"

### What to do with the answers (iterate our design)
- **Signups low** → test tent placement (center-table vs coaster), sharpen the offer copy, train the server line — run a 2-week A/B.
- **Claim rate low** → make the confirmation text a warmer, clearer call-to-action (say the item name, the day, the table-to-claim route), loosen expiry from 7 to 30 days.
- **Table engagement negative** → simplify the tent (one line + QR, no paragraph), trial a coasters-first approach, kill the design if busy tables resent it.
- **Blast response flat** → move the blast time, change the offer style (dollar-off vs buy-one-get-one), or shift from promo to birthday/holiday content for one week to test.
- **Owner time high** → cut the number of approval steps: pre-approve a month of drafts at once, batch check-ins to one per month.
- Capture all answers in `docs/pilot-feedback.md` with a row per venue; aggregate after 6 weeks to decide keep/change/cut before scaling past 3 pilots.

## 7. Ethical Guardrails

1. **Never pitch during peak service.** 2-3 PM Tue/Wed by phone; walk-ins only up front and under 90 seconds. Peak pitch is both rude and wasted.
2. **Never pretend to be from their POS vendor or carrier.** The QR offers must not look like a Square or Toast statement. We say "independent service" in every channel, in writing and on the sign itself.
3. **Full disclosure up front.** New: we are a startup, it's free month one as a pilot, pricing is $99/month after, and they can cancel with seven days' notice. No fine print buried in a contract because there is no contract.
4. **No bundling with false claims.** No "we're working with your Chamber" if we aren't; no "your neighbor signed up" unless they really did; no income or "collecting numbers in week one" guarantees beyond the literal promise (the system is live and collecting in week one).
5. **Consent hygiene.** Every signup carries the SMS consent + disclaimer; guests can STOP easily; we never scrape numbers from reviews, reservations, or the DNC registry.
6. **One ask per touch, three touches max** (call, follow-up, closer), then stop. A polite "no" is a finished sale, not a stage.
7. **Leave the door open without pressure.** Ending message is always "if Mondays change, we're a one-text away" — and then we actually stop contacting them.
