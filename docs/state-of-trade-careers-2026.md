## 1. Executive Summary

**Takeaway 1 — Every one of the 12 trades pays $40K–$100K without a degree.**
The full board on trades.html runs from roofer and automotive mechanic at $40K–$65K up to construction manager at $70K–$100K. Eight of the 12 trades reach at least $70K at the top of their range, and all 12 top out at $60K or more. [VERIFIED]

**Takeaway 2 — The trades pay you to train; college bills you.**
Registered apprentices earn from week one at roughly 40–50% of journeyman scale, with scheduled raises every year. The average bachelor's graduate carries $37,000+ in student loans. The same 4–5 years that leave a graduate in debt leave an apprentice near 80–90% of a licensed wage with zero loans. [VERIFIED]

**Takeaway 3 — Demand outstrips supply.**
Roughly 750,000 open skilled-trade positions sit unfilled in the U.S., with an aging workforce and hiring that "can't be outsourced." HVAC alone accounts for 36,700 openings. [VERIFIED]

**How pay climbs during the paid apprenticeship (electrician, IBEW scale):** Year 1 $17–$22/hr → Year 2 $19–$25/hr → Year 3 $22–$28/hr → Year 4 $25–$32/hr → licensed journey-level $30–$40+/hr ($60K–$80K+). The biggest single raise of the career is the licensing jump; the biggest scheduled jumps land in years 3–4, climbing from ~50–60% of journeyman scale toward 80–90%. Across six trades, year-1 apprentices earn $15–$25/hr, reaching $21–$38/hr by year 4. Four years of $15–$32/hr at full-time hours totals roughly $165K–$215K cumulative take-home for an electrician. ## 4. The Debt Comparison

The site's consistent, verified numbers: a four-year bachelor's degree averages over $100,000 (public in-state) to $241,000 (private), usually with roughly $37,000+ in student loans attached (total U.S. student debt has passed $1.7 trillion across 40M borrowers). By contrast, a trade school program runs $5,000–$15,000 total; community college certificates run $3,000–$10,000; and union apprenticeships cost $0 in tuition while paying you from day one. In the same four years a college student pays tuition and interest, a trade school graduate or apprentice is earning $35,000–$50,000+ — the opportunity cost runs in the student's favor instead of against it. The median U.S. worker earns ~$48,000; the median four-year graduate $60K–$65K — with the eight $70K-capable trades now matching or beating that without the debt. [VERIFIED]

## 6. The Skills Gap

The single headline figure is 750,000+ open skilled-trade positions in the U.S. The site frames it as a labor shortage working in the worker's favor: employers who "can't find anyone" are the ones paying overtime and training costs. Registered apprenticeships are the zero-debt door in — paid training from week one, roughly 8,000 on-the-job hours plus classroom (often 144+ hrs/yr), scheduled raises every 6–12 months, and no student loans at the end. ## 7. Recommendations

- **For students:** start the paid path years earlier than peers — apprenticeships pay from week one; welding can certify in as little as 6 months (6 mo–2 yr certificate) and HVAC in a 2-year program. Watch year-by-year wages, not opening hourly rates; the fastest-rising early wages (union electrician, pipefitter) have the highest-payoff end state.
- **For educators/counselors:** present the trades as a first-line financial option, not a fallback — median electrician pay ($61,590) already outpaces the all-worker median (~$48,000), with no debt attached.
- **For policymakers:** fund and market registered apprenticeships — union paths cost $0 tuition and yield licensed wage earners in 3–5 years; the 750,000 open positions are the scale of the opportunity.
